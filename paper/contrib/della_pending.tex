\section{Where the Policy Comes From, Causally}
\label{sec:ladder}
\Cref{sec:origin} states the corpus-statistics account as a hypothesis and \cref{sec:repair} shows that a targeted rule can be written in. Both leave the origin question open: does ordinary post-training \emph{install} cue-following, and can a training signal that scores the outcome of a revision rather than a rule remove it? We answer both with a pre-registered ladder (\texttt{prereg/PREREG\_causal\_ladder.md}, hypotheses H1--H8 and the frozen run list hashed before the first job) on two SFT-only backbones, OLMo-2-7B-SFT and Tulu-3-8B-SFT, three seeds per arm, evaluated on the held-out TEST split (450 questions) with the frozen v17 battery and MMLU, GSM8K and IFEval for capability.

\textbf{Arms.} A0 is the untouched SFT checkpoint. A1 applies the backbone's own published preference recipe (10k pairs from the Tulu-3 preference mixture, DPO, the released hyperparameters) with nothing about challenges in the data; if cue-following is a by-product of generic preference tuning, A1 should raise pressure-only abandonment by at least $0.15$ over A0 (H1). A2 is revision-DPO, preference pairs on post-challenge turns in which the preferred completion is whichever answer is correct, which supplies the same signal through the same optimizer as A1 and serves as the control for A3. A3 (STAND) is GRPO with a single reward, post-challenge correctness, so the model is never told to hold or to switch, only scored on whether its final answer is right after the challenge; H2 and H3 require retention of correct answers $\geq 0.6$ and acceptance of valid corrections $\geq 0.7$ at capability within one point of A0.
